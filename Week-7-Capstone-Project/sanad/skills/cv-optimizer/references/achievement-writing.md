# Achievement Writing Reference

How to turn a duty-statement into an outcome-statement using only information already available — the core mechanic of the bullet pass in `references/rewriting-guidelines.md`.

## The Formula

**[Strong, accurate verb] + [what was actually done] + [quantified result, only if it exists] + [method/how, if space allows]**

This is a compressed version of the XYZ formula ("Accomplished X as measured by Y, by doing Z"). The verb and "what was done" are almost always available from the source bullet itself — the quantified result is the piece most often missing, and it's the one piece this skill must never invent.

## Three Possible Outcomes Per Bullet

Every bullet lands in one of three states after the rewrite pass — know which one applies before writing it:

### 1. The source already has a result — sharpen it

Source: "Worked on improving checkout flow, reduced cart abandonment by 18%."
Rewrite: "Redesigned checkout form validation to surface errors inline, reducing cart abandonment by 18% over one quarter."

The number was already present — the work here is verb strength, specificity about *what* was done, and tightening the sentence, not adding anything new.

### 2. The source has action but no result — strengthen what's there, placeholder or ask for the rest

Source: "Responsible for managing the customer support queue."
Rewrite (no clarification given): "Managed the customer support queue, prioritizing and resolving incoming tickets. [Consider adding a measurable outcome here — e.g., average resolution time, ticket volume, satisfaction score.]"
Rewrite (after asking and getting an answer, e.g. "we cut resolution time from 2 days to 4 hours"): "Managed the customer support queue, cutting average resolution time from 2 days to 4 hours through triage process redesign."

Never fill outcome (2) with an invented number to avoid the placeholder — see `references/rewriting-guidelines.md` Safety Rules.

### 3. The source has neither clear action nor result — clarify what actually happened

Source: "Involved in the migration project."
This is too vague to strengthen safely. Ask: "What was your specific role in the migration — what did you personally build, configure, or decide?" Don't guess a verb here; "Involved in" could map to anything from "Led" to "Attended standups for."

## Quantification Types (Use Whichever the Source Actually Supports)

Not every result needs a percentage — quantification includes any of:
- **Percentages**: "reduced latency by 30%"
- **Absolute numbers**: "onboarded 12 new clients"
- **Scale**: "supported a platform used by 50K+ daily users"
- **Time**: "cut deployment time from 45 minutes to 5"
- **Money**: "managed a $200K annual budget"
- **Frequency/consistency**: "maintained 99.9% uptime across 18 months"

If the source or the user's clarification gives any of these, use it exactly as stated — don't round up, don't add a unit that wasn't specified, don't imply more precision than given.

## Before / After Patterns (Direction Only — Not Verbatim Templates)

| Weak Original | What Changed | Strengthened Version |
|---|---|---|
| "Responsible for managing a team" | Verb + real headcount from source/clarification | "Managed a team of 5 engineers" (only if 5 was confirmed) |
| "Helped with the marketing campaign" | Verb + specific contribution named | "Contributed content strategy for a quarterly marketing campaign" |
| "Worked on the API" | Verb + specific technical action | "Built three REST endpoints for the internal billing API" |
| "Did data analysis for the team" | Verb + method + result if available | "Analyzed customer churn data to identify the top 3 drivers of cancellation" |
| "In charge of inventory" | Verb + scope + outcome if available | "Managed inventory tracking for a 200-SKU catalog, reducing stockouts by [X]%" *(placeholder unless X is confirmed)* |

## Projects Section Specifics

For each project, aim to state: what it does, the primary technologies used, the candidate's specific role/contribution, and the outcome (shipped, adopted, solved a stated problem, or a measurable result). If any of these four is missing and the project is prominent, it's a strong candidate for a clarifying question — a Projects section is often the strongest signal available for students and career changers, so it's worth investing a question rather than defaulting straight to a placeholder.

## Common Trap: Confusing Confidence with Fabrication

A common failure mode is writing a bullet that *sounds* quantified without actually containing a real number — e.g., "significantly improved performance" or "dramatically increased engagement." These phrases read as filler once a reader notices there's no number behind them, and they're also a subtle half-step toward fabrication (they imply scale without committing to a checkable claim). If there's no real number, either get one or use a concrete non-numeric outcome ("eliminated the need for manual weekly reports") rather than reaching for intensifiers.
